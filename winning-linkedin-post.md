# Anatomy of a Winning LinkedIn Post

A practical guide to writing posts that get seen, read, and acted on.

---

## 1. The Hook (the most important part)

LinkedIn truncates posts after ~2-3 lines with a **"...see more"** link. If the
first lines don't earn the click, nothing else matters.

**A strong hook does one of these:**
- Makes a bold or contrarian claim → *"Most resumes are written to get rejected."*
- Opens a story mid-action → *"I got fired on a Tuesday. By Friday I'd doubled my income."*
- States a surprising result/number → *"This one email got me 40 customer calls."*
- Asks a sharp question → *"Why do great employees quit good managers?"*
- Promises specific value → *"5 things I wish I knew before my first startup."*

**Hook rules:**
- Keep the first line short — one sentence, ideally under 12 words.
- No throat-clearing ("I've been thinking lately about..."). Get to the point.
- Don't give away the payoff — create a curiosity gap that the "see more" resolves.

---

## 2. Formatting for Readability

LinkedIn is read on phones, fast. Walls of text kill reach.

- **One idea per line.** Hit enter often.
- **Use white space** generously between thoughts.
- Keep paragraphs to 1-3 short lines.
- Use line breaks, not dense blocks.
- Lists, arrows (→), and simple symbols help scanning.
- Avoid jargon and long words where a short one works.

> If it looks like an essay, people scroll past. If it looks easy, they read.

---

## 3. Structure That Works

A reliable template:

```
HOOK         → 1-2 lines that stop the scroll
CONTEXT      → quick setup: the situation or problem
BODY         → the story, lesson, or list (the value)
TAKEAWAY     → the one thing you want them to remember
CTA          → invite a response
```

Most viral posts are really just **a story with a lesson** or **a useful list**.

---

## 4. Deliver Real Value

People engage with posts that make them feel something or learn something.

Pick a lane:
- **Teach** — a tactic, framework, or how-to.
- **Story** — a personal experience with a takeaway.
- **Insight** — a fresh perspective on something familiar.
- **Inspire** — a win, a lesson from failure, a mindset shift.

Be specific. *"Increased signups 30% by changing one button"* beats *"marketing is important."*

---

## 5. The Call to Action (CTA)

End by inviting interaction — comments are the strongest engagement signal.

- Ask an open question: *"What would you add?"*
- Invite agreement/disagreement: *"Agree? Or am I wrong here?"*
- Prompt a share/save: *"Repost ♻️ if this helped."*

Keep it to **one** clear ask.

---

## 6. How the Algorithm Thinks

LinkedIn rewards posts that keep people **on the platform** and spark **early
engagement**.

What helps:
- **Dwell time** — longer posts/carousels people actually read.
- **Early comments & reactions** (the first ~60 minutes matter most).
- **Replying to every comment** — each reply re-surfaces the post and adds signal.
- **Native content** — text, images, carousels, video uploaded directly.

What hurts:
- **External links in the post body** — they reduce reach. Put links in the
  **first comment** and say "link in comments."
- Editing right after posting (can suppress reach — edit sparingly).
- Engagement-bait that the platform flags ("comment YES to get my PDF").

---

## 7. Visuals

Posts with visuals consistently outperform plain text.

- **Single image** — relevant, clean, not stocky.
- **Carousels (PDF documents)** — excellent for dwell time and saves.
- **Native video** — strong reach; add captions (most watch muted).
- **Selfie/face photos** — humanize and tend to perform well.

---

## 8. Hashtags, Tags & Mentions

- **3-5 relevant hashtags** is plenty — mix broad and niche.
- **Tag people only when genuinely relevant** (they may comment → more reach).
  Tagging people who don't engage can hurt.
- Don't over-tag; it reads as spam.

---

## 9. Timing & Consistency

- Post when your audience is online — often **Tue-Thu, mornings** (test your own).
- **Be there for the first hour** to reply to comments.
- **Consistency beats virality.** 2-4 quality posts a week compounds over time.
- One post won't build an audience; a habit will.

---

## 10. Voice & Authenticity

- Write like you talk. First person. Conversational.
- Be a person, not a brand. Vulnerability and real stories outperform polish.
- Have a point of view — agreeable-but-bland gets ignored.
- Don't fake it; the audience can tell.

---

## Quick Pre-Publish Checklist

- [ ] Does the first line make me want to click "see more"?
- [ ] Can it be read in one easy scroll (short lines, white space)?
- [ ] Is there one clear takeaway?
- [ ] Did I deliver something useful, emotional, or surprising?
- [ ] Is there a single, clear CTA?
- [ ] Are external links in the comments (not the body)?
- [ ] 3-5 relevant hashtags?
- [ ] Will I be around the next hour to reply?

---

### TL;DR

> **Hook hard, format for skimming, tell a story with one clear lesson, end with a
> question, keep links out of the post, and reply to every comment.** Then do it
> again next week.

## Bonus: Hints to Be a Good Superman

*(Added for testing — a light-hearted aside.)*

- **Lead with the rescue, not the cape.** People remember how you showed up when it mattered, not how you looked doing it.
- **Secret identity = boundaries.** Even Superman needs Clark Kent time. Protect your off-hours.
- **Use your powers in service of others.** Strength is only super when it's pointed at someone else's problem.
- **Know your kryptonite.** Self-awareness about your weaknesses is what keeps them from blindsiding you.
- **Truth, justice, and follow-through.** Big values mean nothing without consistent small actions.
- **You don't have to catch every falling plane.** Prioritize; even heroes can't be everywhere at once.
- **Fly, but stay grounded.** Stay humble and connected to the people you're helping.

> **TL;DR:** Show up for others, guard your energy, know your limits, and let your actions do the talking.
